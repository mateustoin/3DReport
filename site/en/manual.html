Quick manual

From quote to finished sale in 8 steps

This guide covers the essentials to start quoting and selling 3D prints with 3DReport today. For the full list of features, see the home page.

1

Install 3DReport

Download the installer for your operating system (Windows, Linux or macOS) from the releases page and install it as usual.

2

Add your printer

On the Printers screen, add a profile with the energy use (W), the maintenance cost per hour and the machine's investment (price, payback time, days and hours of use). You can add more than one printer and choose which one to use in each quote.

The Maintenance button on each printer keeps what has to be done from time to time (change the nozzle every 200 h, lubricate the rails every 100 h), each with its own interval. The hours come from the Ready or Delivered orders on that printer; calibration, reprints and your own use go in as separate hours. When something is close or overdue, the printer's row warns you. "Done today" logs it in the journal and restarts the counter.

3

Add your filament

On the Filaments screen, enter the brand, the price per kilo, the density and the colors you have (with a manual "In stock" or "Out" toggle for each color).

4

Set up your business

The first time you open it, the app asks four quick questions (your printer and what it cost, the energy price per kWh, the value of your working hour and your profit margin) so it starts calibrated; you can skip them and adjust later. In Settings, set the energy price per kWh, what your working hour is worth, the monthly fixed cost of the business (rent, internet, packaging) together with the print hours of the month, the failure reserve, the tax on the sale (if you pay a percentage; a flat monthly tax goes in the monthly fixed cost), the sales channels you use with the fee of each, an admin cost (for example, modeling time) and the profit margin you want. These values are the same for any printer or quote.

5

Make the quote

The fastest way is to drag the G-code exported by your slicer into the app window: length, time, photo and print settings are filled in for you, and the printer and filament are already selected when the name matches what you saved. If you prefer, on the Quote screen, choose the filament and printer you saved and enter the filament length (in meters, straight from the slicer) and the print time (in minutes). The app works out the production cost and suggests a sale price with your margin. If you entered the value of your hour in Settings, also enter your working time for the whole order, adding it all up: slicing, setting up the plate, taking the print off, removing supports, sanding, painting, packing. To sell several identical parts, enter the quantity: the filament and time numbers stay for one part, and the app multiplies them, showing the total and the price of each unit. If the customer offers a price, type it in the negotiation field: the app shows what is left and warns you if the price does not cover the cost. The app keeps your list price next to the agreed price, and the Dashboard shows how much discount you gave in the period.

6

Save and export

Save the quote (name, photo and model link are optional) and, on the Orders screen, export it as a PDF, copy a short text, open the customer's WhatsApp chat with the quote already written, or make a square image with photo and price to send in a chat or post as a story. When you save, you can pick the delivery date with one click (+3, +7 or +15 days, or a date on the calendar): it stands out in the PDF and the message, and Orders warns you when the date passes. In Settings you can also show the print time in the PDF and the message.

7

Follow the sale to delivery

In Orders, update the order status as the sale moves forward: Quoted → Approved → Printing → Ready → Delivered, and at each change the app offers a ready-made WhatsApp message (you edit the texts in Settings). A quote that has waited 3 days or more is highlighted, with "Resume conversation". The Dashboard counts what the customer approved as a sale and shows, by period, the total sold, the profit, how much each machine hour and work hour earned and the parts that earn the most.

8

Record deposits and payments

In Settings, Orders, the "Default deposit (%)" starts at 0: with a value, every new order is born with the deposit. In Orders, the "Payments" item in the order menu records each amount received, with method and date, shows whether the order is paid, has a deposit or is partly paid, and makes a PDF receipt for each payment. Until the deposit is in, the PDF and the message say how much to pay to start. The tracking code and the notes (yours only, they never go to the customer) are in "Edit details". For sellers in Brazil, there is also a Pix section in Settings, Customer documents, which adds a Pix code and QR to the PDF and the message.

Next steps

After the first quote, explore:

Optional services

Add painting, sanding or delivery in Services and tick which ones apply to each quote, typing how much you will charge on that order. Each service can be charged per part or once per order. Enter the time of the service and the app suggests the value from your hourly rate and shows how much it really earns.

Supplies

Key rings, magnets, screws, boxes, bags: add in Supplies what goes into the part and costs money, and tick in the quote how many go in each part or in the order. The cost goes into the price, like the filament.

Multicolor parts

Printed with an AMS, an MMU or two nozzles? On the Quote screen, click "+ Add filament": each row has the filament, the color and the meters of that material, and each one goes into the price at its own value. Drag in a multicolor G-code from Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer or PrusaSlicer and the rows build themselves, one per extruder, with the usage the slicer reports (purge and tower included). Anything that does not match what you saved stays blank for you to choose. The customer still sees a single item.

Your brand on the PDF, and templates

In Settings → "Customer documents", add your brand name, your logo and your contact (WhatsApp, email, Instagram): they appear in the PDF header, and the name and contact go on the square image too. You can turn on the page border, the watermark and the footer, and click "See how it looks" to check the PDF before saving. The look can be kept as a template; when you load a template, the fields are filled in and you confirm with "Save" (or discard). Logo and contact stay the same when you switch templates. A dark part used to vanish on the blue background of the chat image, so there is a "Image background" option: in Automatic, the app looks at the part's brightness and uses a light background when the part is dark; Dark and Light force one of the two. The PDFs and the chat image carry one small, faded line, "Made with 3DReport", in the bottom corner, far from your name and the price.

Catalog products

No customer yet, or want to keep the parts you offer? Before saving, choose "Catalog product" instead of "Customer order". The product keeps the price, the photo and the files, with no customer, delivery date or shipping, and lives on the Catalog screen, outside the Kanban and the Dashboard. You can advertise a round price ($18.90 instead of $18.37) and give it a category, like Key rings or Decor. When the filament goes up, the card warns that costs changed, and "Update price" shows the before and after. When someone buys, click "Sell": the Quote opens filled in to create the order, and the product stays in the catalog. "Export catalog" makes a PDF with all the products, split by category. If you chose "I am just starting" the first time (or in Settings, under "How you use the app"), the app saves to the catalog by default, and the Dashboard shows which product is worth offering first while there are no sales yet.

Dashboard

On the Dashboard screen, filtered by period, follow the total sold, the profit, the profit per machine hour, how much your work earned per hour (compared with the hour you set), the parts that earned the most and the customers who push the price down the most. A quote the customer has not approved yet stays out of the totals and shows on a separate line, with the conversion rate.

3D viewer for STL

On the Quote screen, attach the model's STL file to view it in 3D (drag to rotate, use the mouse wheel to zoom), capture an automatic photo for the quote and see a suggested difficulty level (Easy, Medium or Hard) to help price more demanding parts. The same field accepts the slicer project's 3MF file: it is kept with the order, and the thumbnail from Bambu Studio, OrcaSlicer or PrusaSlicer becomes the quote photo.
