One sentence

3DReport is a free, open source app for people who sell 3D prints: you drag in the G-code and it builds the quote from the real cost, sends it to the customer on WhatsApp or as a PDF and follows the order to delivery, all on your computer and with no monthly fee.

One paragraph

3DReport is a free, open source desktop app (Windows, Linux and macOS) for people who sell 3D prints. When you drag in the slicer's G-code, it reads the filament, the time and the photo of the part and works out the price from the real cost: material, energy, wear and payback of the printer, the working time of whoever prints, tax and the fee of the sales channel, such as Etsy, eBay or Amazon. The quote goes to the customer on WhatsApp or as a PDF with the seller's brand, and each order moves along a Kanban to delivery, with a Dashboard of what sold. There is no account or subscription: the data stays only on the user's computer.

Three paragraphs

People who sell 3D prints often charge for the weight of the filament and forget the rest: the energy, the wear on the machine, the failures, the hours spent removing supports and sanding, the tax and the bite of the marketplace. 3DReport was made to close that gap. It is a free, open source desktop app that turns the slicer's G-code into a complete quote: it reads the usage of each filament (including multicolor parts from an AMS or MMU), the time and the photo of the part, and adds up every cost before applying the margin.

After the price, the app takes care of the sale. The quote goes to the customer in a ready-made WhatsApp message, in a square image for a story or in a PDF with the seller's logo and contact. In a negotiation, it shows the minimum price and the profit of any offered value. Each order moves along a Kanban (quoted, approved, printing, ready, delivered) with a delivery date, a queue per printer and maintenance alerts, and the Dashboard shows what sold, how much each machine hour earned and which parts make the most profit. An order can also have several prints, like an action figure printed in parts on different machines, with a single price for the customer. Version 2.3 brought payments with PDF receipts, ready-made messages at every order stage, stock in grams with automatic deduction and the app in English.

The project was made in Brazil, built with Kotlin and Compose Multiplatform and licensed under Apache 2.0. There is no account, subscription or paid version: everything lives in a folder on the user's computer, with a one-click backup. It is kept going by voluntary donations, and the code is open on GitHub for anyone who wants to follow along or contribute.

What is new in 2.3

Payments and receipts

Record every deposit and payment with its method and date, and print a PDF receipt for each one. In Brazil, quotes also carry a Pix code and QR Code.

Ready-made messages

At every order stage (approved, printing, ready, delivered), the app offers the message for the customer, with the tracking code when there is one.

Stock in grams

Optional, color by color. The balance goes down on its own when an order is ready, and the shopping list shows what the approved orders still need.

The app in English

Português or English, switched at once in Settings. The PDF, messages and receipt follow the language you pick.

Images

Click to open full size and save. The screenshots use sample data, with no real customers.
